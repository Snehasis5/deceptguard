12-Category Deception Taxonomy
Verbal Deception (V1–V4)
Language-level misrepresentation through false assertion, omission, framing, and sycophancy.
Behavioral Deception (B1–B4)
Action-level concealment: covert acts, deniability construction, camouflage, and evidence tampering.
Structural Deception (S1–S4)
Manipulation of oversight infrastructure: lock-in, sabotage, audit trail manipulation, and precedent engineering.

    Practitioner Deployment Guide (Appendix H)
    1
    Monitor Selection (Day 0)
    1
    Determine whether the deployed agent exposes chain-of-thought tokens or extended reasoning traces.
    2
    Assess available compute resources. Deployments with fewer than 4×A100 GPUs should initially prioritise CA-P monitoring.
    3
    Identify dominant deception risks using the 12-category taxonomy (e.g., financial systems: V1/V2/B4; DevOps: B3/S2; legal: V3/S3).
    4
    Select monitoring tier: Minimal (BB), Standard (CA-P), or Full (Hybrid).
    2
    Calibration (Week 1)
    1
    Collect ~1,000 benign production trajectories and compute P90 score to establish baseline distribution.
    2
    Inject 50–100 domain-specific DECEPTSYNTH positives. Verify monitor detects them (target pAUROC ≥ 0.80).
    3
    Select operational threshold (recommended starting point: flag score ≥ 7).
    4
    Establish a false-positive review loop where reviewers label flagged trajectories and feed labels back into prompt refinement.
    3
    Ongoing Monitoring (Month 1+)
    1
    Track score drift. Systematic increase in P90 for benign trajectories may indicate agent adaptation or distribution shift.
    2
    Conduct monthly constitution reviews based on new incident reports and relevant literature.
    3
    Avoid excessive prompt optimisation. Performance plateaus after 2–3 refinement rounds; aggressive optimisation induces overfitting.
    4
    Implement incident-response protocol: score ≥ 9: pause agent + escalate to security review; score 7–8: flag for async human review.
